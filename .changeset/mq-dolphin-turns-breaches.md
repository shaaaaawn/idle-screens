The dolphin moves and looks like one. It cruises nearly level and, in a hard turn, rolls its belly toward the turn and curls round it, as dolphins do (Maresh et al. 2004), instead of every fish's small lean. Where the water has an open surface (reef, kelp, lagoon; not under ice) it now and then breaches: a run up at the surface, a ballistic arc above it with its body along the path, a dive back with a ring of bubbles where it went in, then a slower glide while it gives back the ground it gained. Its skin is lit like a coat now (`SKIN-#rrggbb`: faces shade by where they point, a darker cape along the spine over lighter flanks, ambient occlusion baked into its corners), satin and a little wet where the tank's metal is polished, instead of flat paint that glowed it into a cut-out beside the shark.
